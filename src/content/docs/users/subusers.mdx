The **Users** tab (titled **Users & Access**) adds subusers: extra accounts with
limited access to your server. Your friend logs in with their own account and
only sees what you allow.

## Invite a subuser

1. Open your server's **Users** tab and find **Invite user**.
2. Enter their panel account email.
3. Pick a permission preset:
   - **Read-only** — view the server, console, files, databases, and alerts.
   - **Power user** — read-only plus power actions, console commands, file
     editing, backups, and database management.
   - **Full access** — every server permission, including scheduled tasks,
     transferring the server, and deleting it.
   - **Custom** — tick exactly the permissions you want to grant; they are
     grouped by area (console, file, server, …).
4. Select **Send invite**.

If email delivery is configured, the panel emails the invite. If it is not, an
**Invite link** dialog appears — select **Copy invite link** and send it to them
yourself. Anyone with the link can accept it with the assigned permissions, so
keep it private.

Invites expire after 7 days. A pending invite can be **Regenerate**d (the old
link stops working) or **Cancel**led. Once accepted, the server appears in their
list.

## Adjust or remove access

The **Active access** section lists everyone with access and their permissions.
For a subuser, toggle individual permission chips and select **Save permissions**,
or select **Remove** to revoke access. Removal is immediate: open sessions lose
access. The owner is marked **Owner** and cannot be removed here.

You cannot grant a permission you do not hold yourself, and you can only manage
subusers on a server you own or administer.

## Least privilege, always

- Every permission is a way to break something. Console access can run
  destructive commands; file access can delete worlds; power access can stop
  the server mid-event.
- Remove subusers who no longer play. Stale access is the most common source of
  "someone griefed my server files" stories.
- Never share your own login instead — then nobody can tell who did what in
  [Activity](/users/activity/), and you cannot revoke access without changing
  your password.
- If you suspect misuse, check **Activity** first so you know what changed.
