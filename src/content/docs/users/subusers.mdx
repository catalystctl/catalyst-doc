The **Users** tab (titled **Users & Access**) adds subusers: extra accounts with
limited access to your server. Your friend signs in with their own account and
sees only what you allow.

## Prerequisites

- A server you own or administer — plain subusers cannot invite others.
- The permissions you intend to grant: you cannot grant a permission you do not
  hold yourself.
- For invites by email, a panel with email delivery configured. Without it, the
  panel shows a copyable invite link instead.

## Invite a subuser

1. Open the server's **Users** tab and find **Invite user**.
2. Enter their panel account email.
3. Pick a preset:
   - **Read-only** — view the server, console, files, databases and alerts.
   - **Power user** — read-only plus power actions, console commands, file
     editing, backups and database management.
   - **Full access** — the full preset: also startup changes, ports, tasks,
     cloning, mods and plugins, transfer, and deleting the server. It does not
     include `backup.pin` ([pinning a backup](/users/backups/#pin-a-backup) is
     reserved for explicit grants).
   - **Custom** — tick exactly the permissions you want; they are grouped by
     area (console, file, server, …).
4. Select **Send invite**.

When email delivery is available the panel emails the invite; otherwise — or if
delivery fails — an **Invite link** dialog appears. Select **Copy** and send the
link yourself. The invite is bound to the email you entered: the invitee accepts
it by signing in with that address, or by creating an account for it on the
invite page. Someone signed in as a different account is asked to sign out
first. Keep the link private — it grants the assigned permissions to whoever
opens it with that account.

Invites expire after 7 days. A pending invite has **Copy link** to share it
again, **Regenerate** (the old link stops working), and **Cancel**. Once
accepted, the server appears in the subuser's list.

## Adjust or remove access

**Active access** lists everyone with access and their permissions. For a
subuser, toggle the permission chips and select **Save permissions**, or select
**Remove** to revoke access. Removal is immediate. The owner is marked **Owner**
and cannot be removed here.

## Least privilege, always

- Every permission is a way to break something: console access can run
  destructive commands, file access can delete worlds, power access can stop the
  server mid-event.
- Remove subusers who no longer play. Stale access is the most common source of
  "someone griefed my server files" stories.
- Never share your own login instead — then nobody can tell who did what in
  [Activity](/users/activity/), and you cannot revoke access without changing
  your password.
